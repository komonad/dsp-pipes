# Rendering

The item icon uses a centered orthographic render of a coaxial straight tube with identical end collars. Its length is about 2.1 times its collar diameter, and its silhouette spans 90% of the tile width. Translucent blue walls retain visible coverage in the 80-pixel inventory atlas. Black/white matting reconstructs straight-alpha pixels from the transparent render. `pwsh tests/run.ps1 -RenderIcon` regenerates `../screenshots/pipe-icon-orthographic.png` from the parametric model in `../tests/PipeIconRender.cs`; `assets-src/fluid-pipe.png` is the embedded source image. `pwsh tests/run.ps1 -IconOnly` checks the embedded icon and native atlas registration.

The valve has an independent item and recipe icon with a silver body, two glass ports and an orange handwheel. The same renderer exports `../screenshots/pipe-valve-icon-orthographic.png`; `assets-src/fluid-valve.png` is its embedded source. Both icon models share glass reflection exposure. `PipeVisuals` configures the world materials.

Procedural meshes follow the actual connections. Tube diameter is 0.56 world units. Thirty-two-sided glass walls have an inward-facing surface at 92% of the outer radius. Collinear segments share a continuous transparent surface. Elbows and multi-branch junctions use opaque steel, with hollow, beveled cuffs where their branches meet adjoining tubes. Junctions use a smooth union of rounded tube shapes. Convex junctions inset their meeting point and curve the branches while preserving tangents at adjoining tubes. Blending fades at ray origins to preserve the outer radius. Free ends have rounded glass heads with steel retaining rims. An isolated node is a short transparent cylinder with two rounded heads; incident connection directions define its regenerated shape. Setting `Appearance.MetalFittings = false` in the BepInEx configuration selects transparent fitting surfaces after a game restart.

One pipe item and one shared glass-wall material serve every fluid. The internal fluid volume carries the color of the stored item. Empty networks show lightly tinted clear walls. Water, oil, acid, hydrogen and deuterium have distinct, uniform fluid colors.

The wall uses the game's generic PBR glass shader with a lightly tinted wall, uniform high smoothness, specular reflection and the storage tank's rim tint. Lower per-surface opacity preserves visibility through both glass interfaces at grazing angles. Storage tank vertex animation depends on tank geometry; the generic glass shader accepts arbitrary pipe meshes. Steel fittings use the Standard metallic shader with 0.98 metalness and 0.86 smoothness. A shared 64-pixel HDR cubemap approximates sky, horizon and ground illumination, scoped to pipe draws through a material property block. The map is generated once per active factory from approximate environmental colors. Opaque fittings include inward-facing triangles with reversed normals so their interiors block the background when viewed through adjoining tube openings. The inner volume uses unlit transparent tint textures, keeping its color independent of surface orientation and sunlight. Shared boundary frames join adjoining glass and fluid rings. Glass interiors render before all exterior walls, including across spatial chunks, to preserve transparent layer order. Glass and fluid use unshadowed transparent passes; steel receives direct lighting and shadows. Standard materials provide wall fallbacks. A component containing fluid displays a uniform, full cross-section whose material is selected by item identity.

Meshes are cached in spatial chunks with frustum culling. Inventory changes reuse the geometry and select materials per network. Connection changes rebuild affected outer chunks and invalidate cached fluid group meshes. The renderer submits cached meshes grouped by spatial chunk.

`../screenshots/pipe-gallery.png` and the individual images are model gallery renders generated by cameras inside the game's Unity runtime, using the same mesh builder and materials as the live renderer.

`../screenshots/ingame` contains offscreen captures of a loaded sandbox planet. Paired layouts place native Conveyor Belt Mk.I entities on the left and water-filled pipe entities on the right, under one game camera and shared world lighting. The scene contains straight runs, elbows, T junctions and elevated runs, with native empty and water-filled tanks in the center for material comparison. Additional matched camera captures show the same pipe network empty and water-filled. A close view compares isolated empty and water-filled nodes. Ordinary belts are empty. The capture harness creates the scene through native factory entity and connection APIs. Interactive placement is exercised by the construction-specific fixtures.

The connection gallery uses a separate 69-node scene. Its far row contains a straight run, elbow and oblique Y; the near row contains a T, vertical inlet and eight-interface junction. Empty and water-filled captures share the same camera pose and geometry.

The structure gallery compares native belts and pipes in serpentine, elevated crossing and loop layouts. The crossing runs occupy separate elevations and components. The belt loop has two incoming branches and a gap between them: 24 vertices, 24 edges and one independent cycle. The pipe layout closes this gap into a theta graph: 25 vertices, 26 edges and two independent cycles. A graph composed solely of native belts has at most one outgoing connection per vertex, giving each connected component at most one independent cycle.

The cube fixture has side length 3 world units, 32 pipe nodes and 36 unit edges along its twelve sides. Its five independent cycles share one inventory. Same-camera empty/filled overview and corner captures, plus steep/low empty views, show vertical connections and overlapping transparent surfaces. Inserting 1,280 water units and extracting them at one node verifies immediate visibility at all 32 nodes.
